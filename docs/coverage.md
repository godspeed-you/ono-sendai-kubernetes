# Coverage

What of [the specification](architecture/kubernetes-provider.md) is built, section by section, with the
evidence for each verdict. Written to be argued with: every row names a module, a test, or the
thing that is missing.

This is not [`STATE.md`](STATE.md). The board says what the last session did and what the next one
should do. This document says where the whole surface stands, including the parts nobody has
started, and it is the only place the untouched sections are counted.

Read as a **snapshot taken on 2026-09-06** against `implementation`. The previous snapshot was
taken on 2026-09-05 and a great deal has landed since; where a verdict has moved, the row says
which way and why. Two rows moved while this was written: `temporal.rs` and `causal.rs` were
absent at the first reading of the tree, at commit `c2f263a`, and landed with `ADR-0020` before
the gate was run. They are counted below as present, because they are — twenty-four modules
compile, `cargo clippy -D warnings` and `cargo doc -D warnings` are clean, and 584 tests pass —
and the §39 and §40 rows were re-read against the code rather than against the announcement. A
coverage map that does not date itself is the second way to be dishonest about state.

---

## The headline

Of the **70 numbered sections**, 6 are implemented, 48 are partial, 12 are built and tested in the
domain library with no route to a user, **none is without code**, and 4 are advisory with nothing
to implement. Of the **7 appendices**, 4 are partial, 2 are built and unreachable, and 1 is
advisory (informative by its own last sentence). Of the **22 core invariants of §4**, 15 hold,
2 hold partly, 5 hold only inside the library, none is vacuous and **none fails**.

Three numbers moved further than any row. The "not started" column went from ten sections to
**zero**: every numbered requirement in the specification now has code somewhere. The invariant
that did not hold — 20, cross-system evidence — now holds, in the library. And the reachability
ratio, which is the number that matters most: **`ono-kubernetes-plugin` imports thirteen of the
twenty-four domain modules**, up from nine of fifteen. `relationship`, `workload`, `place` and
`condition` crossed the boundary, and four of the six things the provider thesis promises are now
things a user can ask for.

The eleven modules no user can reach are `session`, `watch`, `evidence`, `events`, `live`, `logs`,
`budget`, `mutation`, `plan`, `temporal` and `causal` — **10,356 lines with 239 tests**. That is
the largest body of finished, proven, unreachable work in the repository, and it is larger than it
was a day ago, because the library grew faster than the boundary did.

584 tests across the workspace, all green, no live cluster and no network.

## How a section was classified

| Class | Means |
|---|---|
| **implemented** | The requirements are met and tests pin them. |
| **partial** | Some requirements met; the row names the ones that are not. |
| **domain only** | Built and tested in `ono-provider-kubernetes`, with nothing in `ono-kubernetes-plugin` routing it to a user. Where such a section is *also* incomplete in the library, the row says so — the class is decided by reachability first, because an unreachable capability is not a capability. |
| **not started** | No code. |
| **advisory** | Thesis, open questions or informative notes: there is no requirement to implement. Named as its own class rather than filed under "not started", because a section nobody was asked to build is not a gap. |

A `MAY` nobody took is noted as such and is not counted against the section. A doc comment citing
a section number was treated as a pointer, never as proof: every row below was checked by reading
the code the citation leads to, and the class boundary that matters most was checked by reading
`grep -rn "ono_provider_kubernetes::" crates/ono-kubernetes-plugin/src/` rather than by assuming.

## The 22 core invariants of §4

These are the promises the product makes. Fifteen hold, two hold partly, five hold only inside the
library, and — for the first time — none is vacuous and none fails.

## The largest gaps, in the order they block each other

**1. ~~A contributed target is invoked with no options.~~ Closed in core the same day the previous
snapshot was written.** It was read there as the largest gap and it was, until core made
`provider.query` carry the invocation's words through the same parser the command route already
used. Verified again here against the pinned core checkout: `invoke_contributed` now delegates to
`invoke`, which turns `--name value` into JSON arguments, and the `Map::new()` on the `.target.`
branch is gone. Left in place rather than deleted, because a gap list that quietly loses its top
entry teaches a reader nothing about what moved.

**2. ~~There is no session.~~ Written, tested, and not wired.** `session.rs` is 660 lines with 21
tests and holds every one of §6.3's nine components across a call: endpoint, credential, namespace,
discovery, schema cache, negotiated capabilities, cluster fingerprint, watch registry and object
cache. It answers §10.4 by emptying all of them on decisive fingerprint disagreement, answers
§12.4 with four invalidation rules, and answers §20.2 by serving a watched object as a cached
observation. **`ono-kubernetes-plugin` does not import it.** The gap therefore did not close; it
changed shape, from *nothing exists* to *nothing is connected*, and it is still the direct cause of
§50.2's per-query discovery cost and of §20.2 having only one origin to report.

**3. ~~The watch cannot be opened.~~ It can now, and nothing opens it.** The wire driver that was
missing is `WatchDecoder`, and `should_decode_the_frames_of_a_chunked_watch_response_off_the_transport`
drives it the way the real path will: a chunked `200 OK` body off `HttpConnection`, frames split
across chunk boundaries, `WatchEvent`s out, a live stream at the end. A `410` arriving as an error
frame *inside* that successful stream is read as an expiry rather than a generic failure, which is
the case §19.4 is really about. `session.rs` has the registry to hold the streams and `live.rs`
has the view to show them. **No code path in the plugin calls any of it.** That still blocks K3
entirely, Gate F's end-to-end claim, §39's temporal history, §41's live views, §20.3's sync state,
and Appendix A's watch `MUST` for every profile in the matrix — but the blocking reason is now one
route rather than a body of work.

**4. ~~No relationship, place or role reaches a user.~~ Closed.** `k8s-relation` is the route:
one object in, one record per edge out, each carrying the relationship word, both ends as
`place.rs` URIs, the target's roles, and four evidence fields that cannot be dropped. Ten
end-to-end tests cover it, including the two refusals that matter — an inference never becomes a
relationship, and a derivation that could not enumerate is a gap rather than an empty answer. What
did *not* close is the spatial half: these are records, not places in Ono's graph. The package
declares no `contributions.relations` and holds no `relation.write` grant, and core's provider
trait has no place or relation method, so `enter`, `near`, `up` and `map` still do not reach
Kubernetes.

**5. ~~Nothing is exported for a cross-system resolver.~~ Built, and reaching nobody.**
`evidence.rs` reads `spec.providerID`, decomposes it no further than `<scheme>://<path>`, ranks it
above addresses, and is guarded by two tests that read the source rather than trusting a comment.
That was the whole of invariant 20's failure and it is repaired — in the library. §47.7's `MUST`
says cross-system evidence must be inspectable *before* a foreign provider is connected, and no
user can inspect it at all.

The four that are now largest, in the order they block each other:

**6. The session is not wired to the plugin.** Every query still re-resolves the endpoint, re-runs
discovery over every group, and re-fetches the OpenAPI document — three round trips before the
first object. It is the direct cause of §50.2, of §20.2 having one origin, of §12.4's cache having
no reader, of §10.4 having nothing to invalidate, and of §33.2 having no moment at which a
discovery change could be noticed. It is the precondition for gap 7 and it needs no new
Kubernetes knowledge: the state type exists and is tested.

**7. The watch is never opened.** Needs gap 6 to have somewhere to live. Once it is, `live.rs`,
§39, §41, §20.3, K3 and Gate F all follow from one route rather than from six pieces of work, and
`events.rs` joins them as §61.4's sixth requirement.

**8. Four metadata fields reach no user, and they are the last thing K1 waits on.**
`annotations`, `finalizers`, `ownerReferences` and `managedFields` are projected by `object.rs`,
declared by no schema, and excluded from `k8s-resource`'s `other` map because `dynamic::content`
filters `metadata` out by design. §14.5's and §14.6's `MUST`s are unmet at the boundary, and
§61.2's "metadata projection" is therefore the one K1 requirement that is not met — a smaller and
more embarrassing blocker than `get` was, because it is four field arms and four schema entries.
The previous snapshot's board rounded this one up; the §14 row did not, and the two disagreed.

**9. Nothing in eleven of the twenty-four domain modules can be asked for.** `session`, `watch`,
`evidence`, `events`, `live`, `logs`, `budget`, `mutation`, `plan`, `temporal`, `causal` — 10,356
lines and 239 tests. Two of them are unwired *deliberately*: §43.1 puts read usefulness first, and
wiring mutation before the live view is routed would invert §64's order further than it already
is. The other nine are finished work waiting on a target word, and `k8s-relation` is the proof that
adding one is a day's work rather than a phase.

Two smaller things are worth keeping in sight. **§34.2's failure isolation** is a known deliberate
trade recorded on the board, and it currently means one broken aggregated APIService takes the
whole unqualified search down; it is also §48.6 and Appendix D.3, so three rows close together.
**Gate J cannot be satisfied as worded from this side**: the KUANG/11 SDK serves one request at a
time, opening a second `provider.query` before the first is drained quarantines the instance, and
the gate says "concurrently". That is a finding for core, not a gap here, and it is why K0 is
met requirement by requirement and still not claimed.
